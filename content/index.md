Drones and AI have increased the attack surface. The Russo-Ukrainian war and the 2026 Iran war have demonstrated that distance is no longer adequate protection for soft targets. And the various recent hacking incidents have shown that attacks can be scaled with AI.

Currently a $5K drone can cause tens of millions of dollars of damage and $5 of LLM inference can identify exploits in critical systems.

Hybrid attacks utilizing drones and AI hacking techniques will be orders of magnitude more costly.

## Power grid attack scenario

For a few weeks an attacker uses AI to identify exploits in local power companies, local government websites, fire, police, etc.

The attackers monitor the weather forecasts for an extreme weather event that causes high grid load. During that weather event the attackers send multiple preprogrammed drones into 10 substations carrying thick trailing conductive material. The attackers target specific substations in unison to trigger a cascade failure. The aim is to recreate a widespread power outage like the Northeast blackout of 2003.

As the power goes out the attackers use the exploits discovered in local government websites, fire, police, etc. to cause havoc by sending evacuation orders, claiming the water is not safe to drink, asking civilians to take up arms and protect fuel stations, etc.

Then with the roads congested and fire and police departments distracted the attackers trigger wildfires using timed incendiary devices.

The Northeast blackout of 2003 cost between $4 billion and $10 billion and caused almost 100 deaths.

A deliberate attack as described above could be much more costly and the recovery would be slowed by the current transformer shortage in the US.
